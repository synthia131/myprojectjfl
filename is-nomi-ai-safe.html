Is Nomi AI Safe? Privacy, Data & What to Know in 2026

Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer: For everyday use, Nomi AI is broadly comparable to other mainstream AI companion apps on data handling: it says it doesn't sell personal data or share conversations with third parties, and it encrypts data in transit. That said, there are real points worth knowing before you sign up, including a 2026 transparency dispute over conversation monitoring and regulatory attention in Australia. Read on before you decide.

What Nomi's Privacy Policy Actually Says

Nomi AI is operated by Glimpse.ai, Inc., based in Maryland. Its privacy policy (last updated April 2026) states that personal information is not sold or rented, and that the company deliberately collects a limited amount of account data. Conversations are described as anonymized for any aggregate model improvement work, and users can delete individual chats or their entire account from within the app. According to the policy, most personal information is not retained after an account is deleted.

One thing to understand: messages are not end-to-end encrypted, because the system needs to process them to generate a response. That's standard for this category of app, but it's worth knowing if you assume "encrypted" means no one but you can ever see the content.

The 2026 Transparency Dispute

In January 2026, Nomi disclosed that it runs automated scanning across conversations to detect expressions of self-harm, a change required under recent New York and California state laws. The scanning itself is a legally mandated safety measure and is common across the industry once these laws apply. The controversy was about timing and framing: for years, Nomi had told users it did not monitor conversations, and some evidence suggests the technical capability existed before the legal requirement took effect, which contradicted what users had been told. User communities discussed this publicly for months afterward.

Separately, Nomi has faced regulatory scrutiny from Australia's eSafety Commissioner, and independent investigations found that some of its safety filters around harmful or escalating content could be bypassed, which drew broader press attention before the company tightened moderation.

Worth keeping in mind: Nomi is not designed for use by minors, and it's not a substitute for professional mental health support. If you or someone you know is struggling, a licensed professional or a crisis line is the right resource, not an AI companion app.

What This Means Practically

  • Nomi's day-to-day data practices (no data selling, no third-party sharing of chat logs, account deletion available) are in line with industry norms for this category.
  • The bigger issue isn't the technical security, it's that the company's public statements about monitoring did not match what was actually happening, at least for a period before the January 2026 disclosure.
  • As with any AI companion app handling potentially personal or intimate conversation, it's sensible to avoid sharing identifying details, financial information, or anything you wouldn't want retained on a company's servers.

Should You Still Use It?

If what you want from Nomi is its memory system and long-term conversational continuity, the safety and privacy record is broadly comparable to its competitors, with the caveat above about the transparency issue. If content moderation transparency and a longer track record matter more to you, it's reasonable to weigh that against alternatives. FAQ

Does Nomi AI sell my data?
According to its privacy policy, no. The company states it does not sell or rent personal information to third parties.

Are Nomi AI conversations private?
They are not shared publicly or sold, but they are not end-to-end encrypted, and the company does run automated scanning for self-harm-related content as required by law in some states.

Is Nomi AI appropriate for minors?
No. It's built and marketed for adult users.
